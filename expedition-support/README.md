# RAFAK — Remote Area First Aid Kit

The **Remote Area First Aid Kit (RAFAK)** is the emergency medical kit used by the Giovannelli Lab during scientific fieldwork in remote and logistically challenging environments.

RAFAK is designed primarily for **medical emergencies, major injuries, and situations in which access to professional medical care may be substantially delayed**. It complements, rather than replaces, the small personal or team first-aid kits used to manage routine field problems such as minor cuts, blisters, headaches, or other low-severity conditions.

The current RAFAK inventory is provided in `RAFAK-contents.pdf`.

## Intended use

RAFAK was developed for field campaigns in which evacuation or access to a medical facility may require several hours or longer. Its purpose is to provide trained field personnel with equipment and medical supplies that may be required to stabilize an injured or acutely ill team member while professional medical assistance and evacuation are being arranged.

The kit includes material for management of significant bleeding and wounds, basic monitoring and other emergency interventions, together with a limited selection of medications intended for specific medical situations.

**RAFAK is not a substitute for professional medical care.**

## Training is part of the kit

Possessing emergency medical equipment is not equivalent to being able to use it safely.

RAFAK is intended for teams that include personnel with appropriate **remote/wilderness first-aid and emergency-response training**. Some components require specific practical training and should not be used by untrained personnel simply because they are available in the kit.

Training should be refreshed regularly and should include scenario-based practice appropriate to the hazards and remoteness of the planned expedition.

## Medical supervision and telemedicine

Use of prescription medicines, injectable medications, invasive procedures, or other advanced interventions contained in RAFAK should occur **under the direction of a qualified medical professional whenever circumstances permit**.

For remote expeditions, we strongly recommend arranging access to a **24/7 telemedicine or expedition medical support service before deployment**. The service and communication pathway should be established and tested before entering the field, rather than identified after an emergency occurs.

When an emergency exceeds the competence of the personnel present, the operational sequence is:

**stabilize within the limits of training → contact medical support → follow medical direction → initiate or prepare evacuation.**

Loss of communication should be anticipated in the expedition emergency plan and does not expand the scope of practice of field personnel.

## The inventory is not a prescription

`RAFAK-contents.pdf` documents the configuration used by our field team. It is shared as an example and planning resource, **not as a universal packing list, medical prescription, or authorization to administer the listed medications**.

The appropriate contents of a remote medical kit depend on expedition duration, team size, medical history and allergies of team members, environmental hazards, remoteness, evacuation time, local medical infrastructure, available communications, personnel training, and applicable national regulations.

Medication availability, indications, contraindications, formulations, and legal requirements also vary between countries. The kit should therefore be reviewed before each expedition with an appropriately qualified medical professional.

## Before deployment

Before each expedition, the team should:

* review the RAFAK contents with the expedition medical provider;
* confirm that appropriately trained personnel are present;
* establish 24/7 medical or telemedicine support where feasible;
* establish primary and backup communication methods;
* define evacuation procedures and identify appropriate medical facilities;
* review relevant allergies, contraindications, and medical information according to applicable privacy procedures;
* verify medication and sterile-material expiry dates;
* check packaging integrity and replace damaged or compromised items;
* confirm that prescription and controlled medical supplies can legally be transported into and through the countries involved;
* ensure that team members know where RAFAK is carried and who is responsible for it.

The kit should be checked again after any use and restocked before the next deployment.

## Relationship to the field SOP

RAFAK is included in this repository as **expedition-support material**. It is not part of the scientific sampling protocol and does not affect sample collection, metadata, or analytical procedures.

Its inclusion reflects a simpler reality: remote scientific fieldwork requires planning not only for collecting the samples, but also for getting everyone home.

**The most important components of RAFAK are not inside the bag: training, communication, medical support, and an evacuation plan.**
